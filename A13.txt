How to use this file (note for the parent / teacher)
----------------------------------------------------
- There are 32 topics (13.1 to 13.32, the same as in topic.txt), in two
  groups: the Advanced research-level topics (13.1-13.22: algebraic and
  arithmetic geometry, automorphic forms, advanced algebraic and geometric
  topology, quantum topology, mirror symmetry, advanced differential
  geometry, index theory, operator algebras, nonlinear PDE, dynamical
  systems, advanced probability, optimal transport, topological data
  analysis, higher categories, additive combinatorics, geometric group
  theory, mathematics of machine learning, formal verification, quantum
  information and the great open problems) and the Research skills
  (13.23-13.32: reading papers, literature search, choosing a problem,
  examples and counterexamples, writing, presenting, collaboration and
  ethics, teaching, the thesis, and life after the PhD).
  Each topic has:
    * WHAT THE CHILD MUST LEARN   the checklist of definitions, theorems,
                                  standard examples, counterexamples and
                                  methods in that topic
    * PART A — DESCRIPTIVE        12 questions (D1-D12): proofs,
                                  explanations, derivations and worked
                                  numerical problems, each with the answer
                                  and "Teacher explains" (the method step
                                  by step as a teacher shows it on the
                                  board, common mistakes, and a "Now you
                                  try" question)
    * PART B — MATHEMATICAL ONLY  12 questions (M1-M12): pure drills with
                                  answers. Most have many parts (a, b,
                                  c ...) so that EVERY case of the topic is
                                  practised again and again, from the
                                  easiest to the hardest — not just one
                                  example of each idea.
    * TEACHING TIPS               what to do if the student struggles, and
                                  how to make it harder
- In total: 384 descriptive + 384 mathematical questions.
- Level: PhD research. The expectation (topic.txt) is DEPTH IN ONE OR TWO
  of 13.1-13.22 and working AWARENESS of the others: a student should be
  able to do every "Part B" drill in every topic, and every "Part A"
  question in the topics of their own research area.
- The research-skill topics (13.23-13.32) are also practised with
  mathematics: every question there is attached to real computations,
  proofs, estimates or small research tasks, so the skills are learned by
  doing mathematics, not by reading advice.
- What is new in Stage 13: FROM LEARNING TO CREATING. Stage 12 taught the
  student to solve qualifying-exam problems; Stage 13 teaches the student
  to compute invariants in the modern theories, to test conjectures on
  examples, to find counterexamples, and to write and defend new results.
- Conventions: as in A12.txt. N = {1, 2, 3, ...}; log = ln unless a base is
  written; ≅ means isomorphic; □ ends a proof; exact answers first,
  decimals with ≈.
- Method: for every theorem know its hypotheses, the simplest example
  where it applies and the simplest example where it fails. Check every
  computation with software (SageMath, Macaulay2, Magma/PARI, GAP, Lean,
  Python) and write every solution in LaTeX.
- Do 3-4 hours a day: 1-2 descriptive questions (full written proof or
  essay) and 2-3 drills, plus one research paper per week read with the
  method of 13.23.
- Revise Stage 12 (A12.txt) whenever the student finds something
  difficult: 12.14-12.16 before 13.1-13.3; 12.9-12.10 before 13.4-13.9;
  12.2, 12.12-12.13 before 13.10-13.14; 12.21-12.23 before 13.16-13.18;
  12.26 before 13.19; 12.21 before 13.20; 12.27 before 13.21.
