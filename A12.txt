How to use this file (note for the parent / teacher)
----------------------------------------------------
- There are 30 topics (12.1 to 12.30, the same as in topic.txt), in two
  groups: the Core graduate sequence (12.1-12.13: real, functional and
  complex analysis, several complex variables, graduate algebra,
  homological algebra, sheaves, commutative algebra, algebraic topology,
  differential geometry, Lie theory, measure-theoretic probability and
  PDE) and the Specialisation foundations (12.14-12.30: algebraic
  geometry, algebraic and analytic number theory, representation theory,
  harmonic analysis, stochastic calculus, ergodic theory, logic, category
  theory, combinatorics, numerical analysis, optimisation, statistics,
  mathematical physics, mathematical finance, inverse problems and
  algorithms).
  Each topic has:
    * WHAT THE CHILD MUST LEARN   the checklist of definitions, theorems,
                                  standard examples, counterexamples and
                                  methods in that topic
    * PART A — DESCRIPTIVE        12 questions (D1-D12): proofs,
                                  explanations, derivations and worked
                                  numerical problems, each with the answer
                                  and "Teacher explains" (the method step
                                  by step as a teacher shows it on the
                                  board, common mistakes, and a "Now you
                                  try" question)
    * PART B — MATHEMATICAL ONLY  12 questions (M1-M12): pure drills with
                                  answers. Most have many parts (a, b,
                                  c ...) so that EVERY case of the topic is
                                  practised again and again, from the
                                  easiest to the hardest — not just one
                                  example of each idea.
    * TEACHING TIPS               what to do if the student struggles, and
                                  how to make it harder
- In total: 360 descriptive + 360 mathematical questions.
- Level: first-year graduate / qualifying-exam mathematics. The core
  sequence (12.1-12.13) is what a typical PhD qualifying exam covers; the
  specialisation foundations are chosen by research area.
- What is new in Stage 12: SYNTHESIS AND ABSTRACTION. Theorems of Stage 11
  become tools; the student learns to recognise the same structure
  (a functor, a spectral decomposition, a cohomology theory, a
  martingale, a variational principle) in many guises, and to solve
  qualifying-exam problems quickly and completely.
- Conventions: as in A11.txt. N = {1, 2, 3, ...}; log = ln unless a base is
  written; m or λ is Lebesgue measure; a.e. = almost everywhere; ≅ means
  isomorphic; □ ends a proof; exact answers first, decimals with ≈.
- Method: for every theorem know its hypotheses and a counterexample when
  one is dropped; for every computation know which theorem justifies it.
  Use Python/SageMath/Macaulay2/R to CHECK calculations; write every
  solution in LaTeX. Practise past qualifying exams under time limits.
- Do 3 hours a day: 2 descriptive questions (full written proof) and
  2-3 drills per session, plus a weekly timed mixed paper.
- Revise Stage 11 (A11.txt) whenever the student finds something
  difficult: 11.1-11.3 before 12.1, 12.2, 12.12, 12.13, 12.18; 11.4-11.5
  before 12.15-12.16; 11.6-11.7, 11.17 before 12.9-12.11; 11.8-11.9 before
  12.5-12.8, 12.14, 12.17; 11.12-11.13 before 12.12, 12.19, 12.20;
  11.14-11.16 before 12.23-12.30.
