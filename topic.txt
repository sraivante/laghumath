================================================================================
        MATHEMATICS LEARNING PATH: FROM PRE-NURSERY TO PhD
        A sequential list of topics, ordered in the way they build on each other
================================================================================

How to use this list
--------------------
- Topics are numbered and ordered so that each one relies only on topics that
  came before it. Follow the order within a stage; stages are roughly tied to
  age/grade but a learner should move on only after real understanding.
- Grade/age labels are guides, not rules. A strong learner can move faster.
- Principle throughout: concrete -> pictorial -> abstract. Play, objects and
  drawings first; symbols and formal proofs later.
- "Why" matters as much as "how": at every stage ask the learner to explain,
  estimate, find patterns, and check answers in a second way.


================================================================================
STAGE 0: PRE-NURSERY (Age ~2-3)  —  Sensing quantity, shape and pattern
================================================================================
0.1   Same and different (matching objects, pairs of socks, shoes)
0.2   Sorting by one attribute: colour, size, shape, type
0.3   Big / small, tall / short, long / short, heavy / light
0.4   More / less / same (by looking, without counting)
0.5   Full / empty, a lot / a little
0.6   Position words: in, on, under, over, beside, front, behind, top, bottom
0.7   Rote counting songs and rhymes (1 to 5, then 1 to 10)
0.8   Subitising small groups (instantly seeing 1, 2, 3 items)
0.9   Conservation of number (spreading items out does not change how many)
0.10  One-to-one correspondence (one plate per person, one sock per foot)
0.11  Basic shapes by name: circle, square, triangle
0.12  Simple repeating patterns (red-blue-red-blue; clap-stomp-clap)
0.13  Stacking and nesting (blocks, cups) — early sense of size order
0.14  Daily routine sequence: before / after, first / next / last
0.15  Simple puzzles (2-6 piece), shape sorters
0.16  Mazes and following simple directions (spatial reasoning)
0.17  Everyday maths talk: counting stairs, grouping toys, noticing numbers around

STAGE 1: NURSERY / PRE-K (Age ~3-4)  —  Number sense begins
================================================================================
1.1   Counting objects 1-10 with touch-and-count
1.2   Cardinality: the last number counted tells "how many"
1.3   Recognising numerals 0-10
1.4   Matching numerals to quantities
1.5   Zero as "none"
1.6   Comparing groups: more, fewer, equal (by matching and counting)
1.7   Ordering objects by size (seriation: smallest to biggest)
1.8   Shapes: circle, square, triangle, rectangle, oval, star; sides and corners
1.9   3D objects in daily life: ball (sphere), box (cube), can (cylinder)
1.10  Extending and creating AB, AAB, ABC patterns
1.11  Measurement words: longer/shorter, heavier/lighter, holds more/less
1.12  Time words: morning, afternoon, night; today, yesterday, tomorrow
1.13  Early spatial sense: left/right, near/far, inside/outside
1.14  Sorting and simple picture graphs ("How many like apples?")
1.15  Sharing fairly (early idea of division)
1.16  Estimating small quantities ("about how many?") and checking by counting

STAGE 2: KINDERGARTEN / LKG-UKG (Age ~4-6)  —  Numbers to 20 and beyond
================================================================================
2.1   Counting forward to 20, then to 100 (by ones)
2.2   Counting backward from 10, then 20
2.3   Counting on from a given number
2.4   Reading and writing numerals 0-20
2.5   Number words (one, two, ... twenty)
2.6   Ordinal numbers: first, second, third ... tenth
2.7   Number before, after, between
2.8   Ten-frames and five-frames; seeing 5 and 10 as benchmarks
2.9   Composing and decomposing numbers to 10 (number bonds: 7 = 5 + 2 = 4 + 3)
2.10  Making ten (pairs that add to 10)
2.11  Teen numbers as "ten and some more" (early place value)
2.12  Addition as putting together / adding to (with objects)
2.13  Subtraction as taking away / taking apart (with objects)
2.14  Simple word stories for addition and subtraction within 10
2.15  Skip counting by 2s, 5s, 10s
2.16  Even and odd (pairing objects)
2.17  2D shapes: properties (sides, corners), drawing shapes
2.18  3D shapes: cube, cuboid, sphere, cone, cylinder, pyramid
2.19  Composing shapes from other shapes (tangrams, pattern blocks)
2.20  Symmetry in nature and objects (butterfly, leaf)
2.21  Measuring with non-standard units (hand spans, footsteps, paper clips)
2.22  Comparing capacity and weight directly
2.23  Money: recognising coins and notes
2.24  Time: days of the week, months; reading o'clock
2.25  Data: tally marks, pictographs, reading simple charts
2.26  Halves and quarters of shapes and objects (sharing a roti/pizza)
2.27  Board games with dice and tracks (counting on, number sense)
2.28  Simple if-then reasoning and "odd one out" puzzles

3.10  Commutative and associative properties of addition (informally)
3.11  Doubling and halving; adding and multiplying with 0 and 1
3.12  Two-digit addition and subtraction without regrouping
3.13  Two- and three-digit addition and subtraction with regrouping
3.14  Mental math strategies (adding 10, compensation, bridging through 10)
3.15  Estimation and rounding to nearest 10
3.16  Word problems: one-step and two-step
3.17  Multiplication as repeated addition, equal groups, arrays
3.18  Division as equal sharing and equal grouping
3.19  Times tables 2, 5, 10, then 3, 4
3.20  Unit fractions: 1/2, 1/3, 1/4 of shapes and sets
3.21  Fractions of a length on a strip (fair shares that are not whole)
3.22  Length in cm, m (and inches, feet); using a ruler
3.23  Mass (g, kg) and capacity (mL, L)
3.24  Time: half past, quarter past/to, 5-minute intervals; calendar
3.25  Money: counting, making change, simple addition with money
3.26  2D/3D shape properties: faces, edges, vertices
3.27  Angles as turns: quarter, half, three-quarter, full turn; clockwise/anticlockwise
3.28  Lines: straight, curved; open and closed figures
3.29  Patterns in numbers (+2, +5, +10 sequences); hundred chart patterns
3.30  Data: bar graphs and pictographs, asking and answering questions
3.31  Simple logical puzzles and reasoning games
3.32  Abacus / mental arithmetic practice (optional, builds calculation fluency)

================================================================================
HABITS TO BUILD AT EVERY STAGE (the "extraordinary understanding" layer)
================================================================================
- Ask "why does this work?" before memorising "how".
- Represent each idea in multiple ways: objects, pictures, numbers, symbols, words.
- Estimate before calculating; check answers by a different method.
- Look for patterns, make conjectures, then test and prove them.
- Solve non-routine puzzles and problems regularly, not just textbook exercises.
- Explain solutions aloud or in writing to someone else.
- Connect topics: algebra <-> geometry, probability <-> counting, calculus <-> physics.
- Learn from mistakes: keep an error journal.
- Revisit foundational topics at a deeper level (spiral learning).
- Read the history behind ideas and the people who discovered them.
- Enjoy mathematics as play, art and exploration — not only as a school subject.
